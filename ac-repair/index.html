Most AC breakdowns in Dothan trace back to a small part: the run capacitor. It helps start the compressor and condenser fan, loses strength slowly over the years, and tends to give out on the hottest afternoon, which is why so many service calls begin with an outdoor unit that hums while the fan sits motionless. Other frequent culprits include contactors with pitted, burned points, fan motors whose bearings have run dry, slow refrigerant leaks that steadily rob capacity, and evaporator coils that turn into blocks of ice once a dirty filter chokes the airflow.

At Dothan HVAC, every AC repair starts with measurement. Capacitors are metered against the rating on the label. Amp draws on the compressor and fan motor are compared to the data plate. Refrigerant charge is judged by superheat and subcooling, not by pressure readings alone, and any icing prompts a look at the evaporator coil, blower wheel, and filter. This matters because one warm-air complaint can hide four different faults at four very different prices, and a refrigerant top-off that ignores the leak is just a short-term rental on cold air.

Honest advice is part of every visit. If an aging system's compressor has failed, we explain the repair-versus-replacement math plainly and let you decide. Most of the time, though, the fix is simpler, and because our trucks stock capacitors, contactors, fan motors, hard-start kits, and condensate pumps, the house is usually cooling again before we leave. Warning Signs for AC Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Rising electric costs paired with a sticky, uncomfortable house
  • Rapid on-off cycling that never brings the temperature down
  • An iced-over evaporator coil or frozen refrigerant lines
  • Warm or room-temperature air blowing when the thermostat calls for cooling
  • Hissing or bubbling sounds that suggest a refrigerant leak
  • A burning smell or repeated breaker trips when the AC starts
  • Clicking or humming outside with no fan movement
  • Puddles near the air handler or water stains on the ceiling beneath it

The Benefits of Professional AC Repair

Air conditioning repair concentrates several hazards in one cabinet. Capacitors store a charge that bites long after the breaker is off, condensing units run on 240-volt circuits, and the refrigerant loop is a sealed, pressurized system that federal rules do not permit venting to the air; working on it takes gauges, a recovery machine, and a vacuum pump, not a can from the internet. The diagnostic traps are just as real: a frozen coil looks like a refrigerant problem but is just as often an airflow problem, and refilling a leaking system without repairing the leak wastes money while acid and moisture quietly damage the compressor.

Professional service sees the full picture, not just the symptom. Our technicians meter electrical parts before replacing them, make charge decisions from measured superheat and subcooling, locate leaks with electronic detectors and dye, and check the filter, blower, and static pressure any time airflow could be the issue. The result is a repair that lasts the season instead of a guess that fails in three weeks. If your system is not worth saving, we will tell you directly, backed by numbers rather than a sales pitch.

AC Repair Prevention Tips

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Book a professional tune-up each spring before the first heat wave finds the weak parts
  • Rinse the outdoor condenser coil each spring and keep vegetation two feet back
  • Shade is fine, but do not box in the outdoor unit with fences or covers
  • Pay attention to buzzing or clicking when the AC starts, a common warning of a failing capacitor
  • Leave vents open in every room, since closing them strains the blower
  • Swap filters monthly while the AC is running hard to keep the evaporator coil from icing
  • Flush the condensate drain line with the season's first use to head off clogs
