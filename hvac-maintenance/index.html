The Benefits of Professional HVAC Maintenance

Filters, a clear outdoor unit, open registers, and a quick look at the drain are the homeowner's share of maintenance, and they matter. Beyond those, it takes test instruments. Failures that leave you without heat or air rarely look like anything: an 80 percent capacitor resembles a healthy one, and a low charge is visible only in superheat and subcooling. A cracked heat exchanger, a lazy flame signal, a pressure switch near its limit, or an amp draw drifting toward the breaker rating all stay hidden until someone measures them, and each is cheaper to fix a season early.

On fuel-burning equipment, maintenance is also about safety. A failing burner, flame proving circuit, vent, or draft problem can put combustion products where they do not belong, which is why those items get instrumented verification every year. How to Prevent HVAC Maintenance Problems

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Leave every supply and return register open, including those in unused rooms
  • Replace your filter on a one- to three-month cycle; nothing else you do pays off more
  • Flush the condensate line every spring so algae cannot build up and clog it
  • Schedule maintenance in early spring and early fall, ahead of the busy season and the extreme weather
  • Test carbon monoxide detectors monthly if any of your equipment burns fuel
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of vegetation and rinse the coil gently each spring
  • Jot down any unusual sounds or run times, since they help your technician diagnose early
